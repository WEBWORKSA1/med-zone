## Where ideal weight formulas come from

Ideal body weight (IBW) formulas were created for medicine, not fitness. Dr. B.J. Devine published his in 1974 to calculate doses of drugs that distribute mainly into lean tissue. Robinson, Miller and Hamwi later proposed variations. All four start from a base weight at 5 feet (152.4 cm) and add a fixed amount for every inch above that.

## The four formulas (kg, inches over 5 ft)

| Formula | Men | Women |
|---|---|---|
| Devine | 50 + 2.3 per inch | 45.5 + 2.3 per inch |
| Robinson | 52 + 1.9 per inch | 49 + 1.7 per inch |
| Miller | 56.2 + 1.41 per inch | 53.1 + 1.36 per inch |
| Hamwi | 48 + 2.7 per inch | 45.5 + 2.2 per inch |

## Which number should you use?

For everyday health goals, the **healthy-BMI weight range** (BMI 18.5 to 24.9 for your height) is the more useful target, and the calculator shows it next to the formulas. Clinicians still use Devine IBW for some drug doses, ventilator settings and the [creatinine clearance calculator](../creatinine-clearance-calculator/).

## Limits

None of these formulas considers frame size, muscle mass, age or ethnicity, and none was derived from health outcomes. They become unreliable for adults shorter than 5 feet.
