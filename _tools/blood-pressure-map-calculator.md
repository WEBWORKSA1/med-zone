## What MAP measures

Mean arterial pressure is the average pressure pushing blood into your organs over one heartbeat. Because the heart spends longer relaxing than contracting, MAP sits closer to the diastolic number. Intensive-care teams watch it closely: a MAP of at least 65 mmHg is a common target to keep the brain, heart and kidneys supplied.

<span class="formula">MAP = diastolic + (systolic - diastolic) / 3</span>

## Blood pressure categories (ACC/AHA 2017)

| Category | Systolic | | Diastolic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal | below 120 | and | below 80 |
| Elevated | 120 to 129 | and | below 80 |
| Stage 1 hypertension | 130 to 139 | or | 80 to 89 |
| Stage 2 hypertension | 140 or higher | or | 90 or higher |
| Hypertensive crisis | above 180 | and/or | above 120 |

A reading above 180/120 with chest pain, shortness of breath, weakness, vision change or difficulty speaking is an emergency: call your local emergency number.

## Pulse pressure

The gap between systolic and diastolic is the pulse pressure. A persistently wide pulse pressure in older adults often reflects stiffer arteries.

## Getting an accurate reading

Sit for 5 minutes with your back supported and feet flat, arm resting at heart level, no caffeine or exercise for 30 minutes beforehand. Use a validated upper-arm cuff of the right size and average two readings a minute apart.
