## How your due date is estimated

Pregnancy is counted from the first day of your last menstrual period (LMP), about two weeks before conception. Naegele's rule adds 280 days (40 weeks) to the LMP. If your cycle is longer or shorter than 28 days, ovulation usually shifts by the same amount, so the calculator moves the date accordingly.

## Key milestones

| Stage | Gestational age |

| First trimester | Conception to 13 weeks 6 days |
| Second trimester | 14 weeks 0 days to 27 weeks 6 days |

| Early term | 37 weeks 0 days to 38 weeks 6 days |
| Full term | 39 weeks 0 days to 40 weeks 6 days |
| Late term | 41 weeks 0 days to 41 weeks 6 days |

## How accurate is it?

A due date is an estimate. Most babies arrive within about two weeks either side of it. An ultrasound in the first trimester measures the embryo directly and is the most accurate way to date a pregnancy; if it differs enough from the LMP date, your care team will use the ultrasound date.

## Next steps

If you haven't yet, book a prenatal visit, start a daily prenatal vitamin with folic acid, and check your coverage for maternity care.
