## What BMI tells you

Body mass index compares your weight with your height. It is quick, free and the same worldwide, which is why doctors and insurers use it as a first screen for weight-related health risk. It is not a diagnosis: it cannot tell muscle from fat or show where fat sits on the body.

## How it's calculated

<span class="formula">BMI = weight (kg) / height (m)²</span>. In US units the same result comes from <span class="formula">703 × weight (lb) / height (in)²</span>.

## Adult BMI categories

| BMI | Category |
|---|---|
| Below 18.5 | Underweight |
| 18.5 to 24.9 | Healthy weight |
| 25 to 29.9 | Overweight |
| 30 to 34.9 | Obesity, class 1 |
| 35 to 39.9 | Obesity, class 2 |
| 40 or higher | Obesity, class 3 |

## When BMI misleads

- **Athletes and muscular builds** can land in the overweight range with little body fat.
- **Older adults** lose muscle with age, so a normal BMI can hide excess fat.
- **Some Asian populations** carry higher risk at lower BMIs; several Asian health agencies use 23 and 27.5 as action points.
- **Children and teens** need BMI-for-age percentiles, not adult cut-offs.

For a fuller picture, pair BMI with your [waist-to-height ratio](../waist-to-height-ratio/), which tracks the abdominal fat linked to heart disease and type 2 diabetes.
