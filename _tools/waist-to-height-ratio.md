## The one-line rule

**Keep your waist to less than half your height.** That simple message, now part of UK NICE guidance, captures what the ratio measures: fat stored around the abdominal organs, which is closely linked to type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure and heart disease.

## How it's calculated

<span class="formula">Ratio = waist circumference / height</span>, using the same unit for both.

| Ratio | What it suggests |
|---|---|
| Below 0.4 | Below the usual range; check that your weight is not too low |
| 0.4 to 0.49 | Healthy range |
| 0.5 to 0.59 | Increased health risk |
| 0.6 or higher | High health risk |

## Why it complements BMI

Two people with the same BMI can carry fat very differently. Waist-to-height ratio works across sexes and most ethnic groups with the same cut-off, which makes it easy to use and to track over time.

## Measuring tips

Stand relaxed, feet together, and measure on bare skin. Don't suck in. Take two measurements and average them.
