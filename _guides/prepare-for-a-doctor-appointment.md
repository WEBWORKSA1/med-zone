Primary-care visits are short, often 15 to 20 minutes, and much of what's said is forgotten soon after. Ten minutes of preparation can make the difference between leaving confused and leaving with a plan.

## Before the visit

**Write down your main concern in one sentence.** For example: "I've had headaches most mornings for three weeks and painkillers barely help." Lead with it. Concerns raised at the end of a visit ("oh, and one more thing") often get the least time.

**Note the story of the symptom.** When did it start? How often? What makes it better or worse? What have you tried? Rate it from 0 to 10.

**List every medicine.** Include prescriptions, over-the-counter drugs, vitamins and supplements, with doses. A photo of each label works. Bring your allergy list too.

**Gather numbers.** Home blood pressure readings, glucose logs, a weight trend or a symptom diary are far more useful than memory.

**Request records** from other clinicians in advance if you're seeing someone new.

Our [visit question builder](../../learn/visit-prep/) puts all of this on one printable page, without sending anything over the internet.

## Questions worth asking

- What do you think is causing this, and what else could it be?
- What tests do I need, and what will they change?
- What are my options, including waiting or doing nothing?
- What are the side effects and costs?
- Which warning signs mean I should come back sooner or go to emergency?
- When and how will I get my results?

Studies of "question prompt lists" like this one find that patients who use them ask more questions and often feel better informed, without making appointments longer.

## During the visit

- **Bring someone** if the visit is important. A second set of ears helps.
- **Take notes**, or ask whether you can record the key points.
- **Be specific and honest**, including about alcohol, smoking, drugs, sexual health and whether you actually take your medicines. Clinicians have heard it all and need accurate information.
- **Use the teach-back method:** repeat the plan in your own words. "So I'm starting this tablet once a day, getting a blood test in two weeks, and coming back if the pain gets worse."

## After the visit

- Check your online portal for the visit summary and results.
- Book follow-up tests and appointments before you forget.
- If something is unclear, send a message. A short question now prevents a bigger problem later.

## Telehealth tips

For video visits, test your device in advance, find a quiet, well-lit spot, have your medicine list and a thermometer or blood pressure cuff nearby, and keep a phone number handy in case the connection drops.
