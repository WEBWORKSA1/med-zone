Your kidneys filter about 180 litres of blood plasma a day, balancing water, salt, potassium and acid, and making hormones that control blood pressure and red blood cells. Kidney disease is common and usually silent until late, so the blood and urine tests that catch it early matter.

## Two tests, two questions

**eGFR: how well are the kidneys filtering?** Labs estimate glomerular filtration rate from your creatinine level, age and sex. Since 2021, the widely used CKD-EPI equation no longer includes race. Try it with our [eGFR calculator](../../tools/egfr-calculator/).

**Urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (ACR): are the filters leaking?** Healthy kidneys keep albumin, a protein, in the blood. Albumin in the urine is often the earliest sign of damage, especially in diabetes and high blood pressure. Below 30 mg/g (3 mg/mmol) is normal.

Doctors need both: someone can have a normal eGFR but leaking filters, or a lowish eGFR that is stable and harmless with age.

## How chronic kidney disease is staged

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) means an eGFR below 60, or signs of kidney damage such as albuminuria, lasting at least three months. It is staged by both numbers:

| Stage | eGFR | |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | 90 or more | Normal, but damage present |
| G2 | 60 to 89 | Mildly decreased |
| G3a | 45 to 59 | Mildly to moderately decreased |
| G3b | 30 to 44 | Moderately to severely decreased |
| G4 | 15 to 29 | Severely decreased |
| G5 | Below 15 | Kidney failure |

Albuminuria adds an "A" category (A1 normal, A2 moderately increased, A3 severely increased). Together they predict the risk of kidney failure and of heart disease, which is actually the more common outcome.

## Who should be tested

People with diabetes, high blood pressure, heart disease, a family history of kidney failure, or a past episode of acute kidney injury should have eGFR and urine ACR checked regularly, usually at least yearly.

## Why a single result can mislead

Creatinine comes from muscle, so very muscular people may show a falsely low eGFR, and frail people a falsely high one. Dehydration, a large meat meal and some medicines can shift creatinine temporarily. During sudden illness eGFR is unreliable. That is why CKD needs confirmation over three months.

## Protecting your kidneys

- **Control blood pressure**, often to below 130/80 when CKD is present.
- **Manage blood sugar** if you have diabetes.
- **Ask about kidney-protective medicines.** ACE inhibitors or ARBs, and SGLT2 inhibitors, slow CKD progression in many people.
- **Be careful with NSAIDs** such as ibuprofen and naproxen; use them only as advised.
- **Check doses.** Many medicines need adjustment when kidney function is low; pharmacists often use the [Cockcroft-Gault clearance](../../tools/creatinine-clearance-calculator/).
- **Don't smoke**, stay active and keep a healthy weight.

## Questions to ask

- What are my eGFR and urine ACR, and how have they changed?
- What stage is my kidney function, and what is my risk?
- Do any of my medicines need a dose change?
- Would a kidney specialist referral help?
