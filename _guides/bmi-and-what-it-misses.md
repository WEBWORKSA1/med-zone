Body mass index is the most-used number in weight-related health. It is also one of the most misunderstood. Used for what it is good at, it is helpful; used as a verdict on an individual body, it can mislead.

## What BMI is

BMI is your weight in kilograms divided by your height in metres squared. Adults are grouped as underweight (below 18.5), healthy weight (18.5 to 24.9), overweight (25 to 29.9) and obesity (30 and above). Try it with our [BMI calculator](../../tools/bmi-calculator/).

The formula dates from the 1830s and was designed to describe populations, not individuals. It became popular in medicine because it is free, quick and links reasonably well with body fat across large groups of people.

## What it does well

- **Screening.** Higher BMI ranges are associated with higher average risk of type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, sleep apnea, fatty liver disease and some cancers.
- **Tracking.** For one person over time, a rising or falling BMI mostly reflects real weight change.
- **Comparison.** Researchers and health agencies can compare countries and decades using the same measure.

## Where it misleads

**It can't tell muscle from fat.** A muscular athlete can have a BMI of 28 with little body fat.

**It ignores where fat sits.** Fat around the abdominal organs (visceral fat) drives far more metabolic risk than fat on the hips and thighs. Two people with identical BMIs can have very different risk.

**It changes meaning with age.** Older adults lose muscle, so a "healthy" BMI can hide a high body-fat percentage.

**It differs by ancestry.** People of South Asian, Chinese and some other Asian backgrounds tend to develop type 2 diabetes at lower BMIs. Several health agencies use lower action points, such as 23 and 27.5.

**It doesn't apply to children** in the same way. Kids are assessed with BMI-for-age percentiles.

## Better together: three quick measures

1. **Waist-to-height ratio.** Keep your waist to less than half your height. It captures abdominal fat and works with one cut-off across sexes. Use the [waist-to-height calculator](../../tools/waist-to-height-ratio/).
2. **Body-fat estimate.** The [U.S. Navy tape method](../../tools/body-fat-calculator/) gives a rough body-fat percentage at home.
3. **Metabolic markers.** Blood pressure, fasting glucose or A1C, and a lipid panel show how your body is actually handling its weight.

## Health is more than a number

Fitness, sleep, eating pattern, smoking, alcohol and stress shape health independently of weight. Research consistently shows that physically fit people at higher BMIs can have lower risk than unfit people at lower BMIs. If you want to change your weight, aim for habits you can keep, and use the numbers to track progress rather than to judge yourself.

If you are considering medical weight-loss treatment, ask about your BMI together with your waist measurement, blood tests and any weight-related conditions; most guidelines use all of these to decide who benefits.
