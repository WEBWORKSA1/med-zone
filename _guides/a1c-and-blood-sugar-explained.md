Blood sugar numbers come in different units, from different tests and with different cut-offs, which makes them confusing. Here is how they fit together.

## The three diagnostic tests

| Test | Normal | Prediabetes | Diabetes range |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1C | below 5.7% | 5.7 to 6.4% | 6.5% or higher |
| Fasting glucose | below 100 mg/dL (5.6 mmol/L) | 100 to 125 | 126 or higher (7.0) |
| 2-hour glucose tolerance test | below 140 mg/dL (7.8) | 140 to 199 | 200 or higher (11.1) |

A diagnosis of diabetes normally needs two abnormal results, either the same test repeated or two different tests, unless someone has classic symptoms and a very high glucose.

## What A1C actually measures

Glucose attaches to hemoglobin in red blood cells. Red cells live about three months, so the percentage of glycated hemoglobin (A1C) reflects average glucose over that time. Our [A1C calculator](../../tools/a1c-calculator/) converts it into the estimated average glucose you'd see on a meter: 7% is about 154 mg/dL (8.6 mmol/L).

Because A1C is an average, two people with the same A1C can have very different days: one steady, one swinging between highs and lows. Continuous glucose monitors show this "time in range" directly.

## When A1C can mislead

- **Anemia and blood loss** change red-cell lifespan and can push A1C down or up.
- **Hemoglobin variants**, such as sickle-cell trait, can interfere with some lab methods.
- **Pregnancy** lowers A1C, so glucose tolerance testing is preferred.
- **Advanced kidney disease** and recent transfusions also reduce accuracy.

When A1C and finger-stick readings don't agree, ask whether one of these applies.

## mg/dL or mmol/L?

The United States uses mg/dL; Canada, the UK, Australia and much of the world use mmol/L. Divide mg/dL by 18 to get mmol/L, or use our [glucose converter](../../tools/glucose-converter/).

## Targets for people with diabetes

Many adults with diabetes aim for an A1C below 7%, but targets are personal. Looser targets may be safer for older adults or people prone to low blood sugar; tighter targets may suit younger people early in the disease if they can be reached safely.

## Prediabetes is a chance to act

Prediabetes raises the risk of type 2 diabetes and heart disease, but it is often reversible. In the landmark Diabetes Prevention Program, modest weight loss (about 7 percent of body weight) and 150 minutes of activity a week lowered the chance of developing diabetes by more than half over about three years.

## Recognizing a low

If you take insulin or sulfonylureas, learn the signs of hypoglycemia: shakiness, sweating, hunger, a racing heart, then confusion. Below 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L), take 15 grams of fast-acting sugar, recheck in 15 minutes and repeat if needed. Severe lows are an emergency.
