Heart rate is a free, honest gauge of how hard you're working. Training by zones helps you go easy on easy days, hard on hard days and avoid the grey middle where most people get stuck.

## Step 1: estimate your maximum

The classic rule is 220 minus your age. A large analysis by Tanaka and colleagues found a better fit: **208 minus 0.7 times your age**. For a 50-year-old, that's 173 bpm. Both are averages; your true maximum can be 10 or more beats higher or lower. A supervised exercise test gives the real number.

## Step 2: know your resting rate

Count your pulse for a full minute after waking, before getting up, on a few mornings. A lower resting rate usually reflects better fitness. Entering it in our [zone calculator](../../tools/heart-rate-zones/) uses the Karvonen method, which tailors zones to your heart-rate reserve.

## The five zones

| Zone | % of max | Feels like | Builds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 50 to 60 | Very easy | Recovery |
| 2 | 60 to 70 | Easy, can chat | Aerobic base, endurance |
| 3 | 70 to 80 | Moderate, short sentences | Aerobic capacity |
| 4 | 80 to 90 | Hard, few words | Lactate threshold |
| 5 | 90 to 100 | All-out | Speed, peak power |

## How to use zones

- **Build a base in zone 2.** Many coaches suggest that most weekly training time (often around 80 percent) is easy, with a smaller share hard.
- **Make hard sessions count.** Short intervals in zones 4 and 5, with full recovery between, drive big fitness gains.
- **Use the talk test** when you have no monitor: if you can talk but not sing, you're at moderate intensity.

## How much exercise?

Adults should get at least 150 minutes a week of moderate activity or 75 minutes of vigorous activity, plus muscle strengthening on two or more days. Any movement beats none.

## Safety first

Beta-blockers and some other medicines lower heart rate, making standard zones misleading; use perceived effort instead and ask your clinician. Stop and seek help for chest pain or pressure, fainting, unusual breathlessness or a racing, irregular heartbeat. If you have heart disease, diabetes or kidney disease, or haven't exercised in a long time, check with your clinician before starting vigorous exercise.

## About wearables

Wrist-based optical sensors are reasonably accurate at steady effort but can lag during intervals. A chest strap is more accurate for interval training.
