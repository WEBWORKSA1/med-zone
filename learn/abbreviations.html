Why some abbreviations are banned

Handwritten or rushed abbreviations have caused serious harm. "U" for units has been misread as a zero, turning 10 units of insulin into 100. "QD" (daily) and "QOD" (every other day) have been confused with each other. A trailing zero ("1.0 mg") can be read as 10 mg when the decimal point is missed.

The Joint Commission's official "Do Not Use" list and the Institute for Safe Medication Practices' list of error-prone abbreviations ask clinicians to write these terms out in full. If you see one on your prescription, it is reasonable to ask the pharmacist to confirm what it means.
