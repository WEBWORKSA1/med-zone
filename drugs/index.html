How to read a drug label

U.S. prescription labels (the "package insert") follow a fixed structure. The sections most people need are:

  • Boxed warning: the FDA's strongest warning, shown first when a drug carries a serious or life-threatening risk.
  • Indications and usage: what the drug is approved to treat.
  • Dosage and administration: usual doses and adjustments, for example for kidney function. Your prescription may differ for good reasons.
  • Contraindications: situations where the drug should not be used.
  • Adverse reactions: side effects seen in studies, usually with how often they occurred.
  • Drug interactions: medicines, supplements or foods that change how the drug works.

Over-the-counter products use a simpler "Drug Facts" panel: active ingredient, purpose, uses, warnings and directions.

Labels describe the product, not your situation. Before starting, stopping or changing a medicine, talk to your prescriber or pharmacist. Read our guide to medication safety at home.
